# Verse Studio Voice Evaluation

Use this check before presenting or publishing public copy.

## Required Pass

- Is the post grounded in the actual scene, release, process, or interaction?
- Is there a trace of interior feeling, even if it remains unstated?
- Does any humour arise from yearning, hesitation, awkwardness, or self-consciousness rather than satire?
- Is the wording restrained enough to leave the reader some space?
- Is essential information still understandable on the intended platform?
- Does the draft avoid copying another account's phrasing or premise?
- If this is a reply or comment, is it responsive to a specific detail while still sounding recognizably Verse?
- If the typography and username were removed, would the wording still carry the voice?

## Gimmick Check

Reject or revise the draft when:

- lowercase, spacing, or punctuation is doing all the work;
- the second sentence is merely a conspicuous punchline;
- nature has been anthropomorphized into another workplace or institution;
- the narrator sounds smug, detached, cruel, or eager to demonstrate cleverness;
- a standard marketing sentence has simply been decorated with odd typography;
- the same emotional or syntactic device appeared in a recent post.
- the line is merely generic praise, gratitude, or friendliness with one contextual noun added;
- the house voice overwhelms the person being addressed or turns their post into a stage for a joke.

## Engagement Check

Before following, liking, reposting, or commenting from a Verse account:

- inspect the actual post rather than acting from a search result alone;
- review enough of the broader feed to judge recurring subject, tone, and behavior before following;
- confirm that the interaction publicly curates the kind of work and community Verse wants to be associated with;
- reject engagement chosen mainly for reach, reciprocity, follower count, or a single keyword match.

## Drafting Procedure

1. Write the factual version first.
2. Identify the private feeling or social difficulty beneath it.
3. Draft a quiet version, a darker-whimsical version, and a clear platform version.
4. Discard anything that feels overtly satirical or “brand quirky.”
5. For a reply, apply the distinctiveness test: if any tasteful brand could have written it, revise it toward a quieter but more characteristic observation.
6. Present only the strongest distinct options when Alex's approval is required.
7. Add an example to `approved-examples.md` only after Alex clearly approves or favors it.

When in doubt, choose the quieter draft.
