# Release Launch Playbook

## 1. Intake

Record:

- release name and campaign ID;
- format, duration, aspect ratio, and file location;
- artistic intent and honest audience promise;
- music and visual provenance;
- primary goal and explicit non-goals;
- target destination and release date;
- available master, thumbnails, excerpts, captions, and process material;
- decisions needed from Alex and current approval status.

Inspect the actual media before final packaging. Do not infer technical facts from a filename or a rough description.

## 2. Campaign Shape

Build a path instead of a pile of posts:

```text
native excerpt or recommendation -> canonical full work -> subscription or return visit
```

Give each asset one job:

- **Primary release:** deliver the complete artistic experience.
- **Excerpts:** earn attention with a complete miniature and direct interested viewers to the full work.
- **Process material:** establish authorship and craft.
- **Curator outreach:** add trusted context for a well-matched audience.
- **Paid test:** test an audience or packaging hypothesis after baseline data exists.

## 3. Packaging

Prepare up to three honest title and thumbnail hypotheses when the platform supports testing. Optimize for the right click, not the largest possible click-through rate.

For each public draft:

- use the vocabulary the intended audience actually uses;
- keep the title specific about setting, format, music, or use case;
- make the first two description lines intelligible without expanding the description;
- credit creative direction, editing, music, and generation tools accurately;
- avoid keyword clouds, unrelated trend terms, and promises the work does not fulfill;
- mark `draft`, `approved`, `scheduled`, or `published` in the campaign record.

## 4. Default Timeline

Adapt to the release rather than posting mechanically.

- **Before release:** capture baseline, inspect media, finalize destination, package the upload, prepare tracking, and inventory excerpts.
- **Launch day:** publish the primary work, verify the public page, record URL and initial state, and make only the strongest approved supporting announcement.
- **Days 1–3:** publish one or two native excerpts, respond to meaningful early engagement, and check for packaging mismatch rather than overreacting to small samples.
- **Days 4–7:** publish another distinct excerpt or process angle, begin selective community or curator outreach, and take the 7-day snapshot.
- **Days 8–14:** use evidence to choose the next excerpt, packaging revision, collaboration, or small paid test.
- **Day 30:** close the first campaign cycle with a concise retrospective and a recommendation for the next release.

## 5. Approval Gates

Keep these independent:

1. Channel or account creation and live profile changes.
2. Final title, description, captions, hashtags, pinned comments, and About copy.
3. Upload visibility and final publication.
4. Outreach messages and commitments to third parties.
5. Paid budget, targeting, campaign creative, and launch.

A direct instruction to perform a named action counts as approval for that action, but does not grant open-ended authority for the others.
